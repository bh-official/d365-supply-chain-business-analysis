# Stakeholder Analysis: Supply Chain Process Improvement

## 1. Purpose

This document identifies the stakeholders involved in improving procurement, inventory management and supplier invoice processing for a fictional manufacturing company.

The objective is to understand each stakeholder's responsibilities, concerns, information needs and involvement in the proposed business change.

## 2. Stakeholder Register

| Stakeholder | Responsibilities | Main Concerns | Involvement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Procurement Manager | Oversees purchasing and supplier relationships | Purchase order visibility and supplier delays | Defines purchasing requirements and approves process changes |
| Procurement Officer | Creates and manages purchase orders | Manual work and order status updates | Explains current purchasing procedures and validates requirements |
| Warehouse Manager | Oversees goods receipt and stock control | Inventory accuracy and receiving delays | Defines warehouse processes and validates proposed improvements |
| Warehouse Operative | Receives, checks and records goods | Ease of use and accurate transaction recording | Participates in process observation and UAT |
| Finance Manager | Oversees supplier payments and financial controls | Invoice discrepancies and payment accuracy | Defines invoice matching rules and financial controls |
| Accounts Payable Officer | Processes supplier invoices | Manual investigation and delayed approvals | Explains invoice processing and tests exception scenarios |
| Supply Chain Manager | Monitors overall supply chain performance | Delivery performance, operational efficiency and reporting | Agrees business priorities and reviews KPIs |
| IT / ERP Team | Supports the ERP system and technical delivery | System configuration, security, integrations and support | Assesses feasibility and supports solution delivery |
| Suppliers | Deliver goods and submit invoices | Clear orders, delivery expectations and timely payment | Provide information about external process issues where appropriate |

## 3. Stakeholder Analysis

The Procurement, Warehouse and Finance teams are key stakeholders because the proposed changes affect their daily activities.

The Supply Chain Manager is an important decision maker because the improvements must support wider operational objectives.

The IT / ERP team should be involved early to assess whether the proposed requirements can be met using existing system capabilities, configuration or other changes.

Suppliers are external stakeholders. Their involvement should be proportionate to the changes and may be required if supplier communication or invoicing processes are affected.

## 4. Communication Plan

| Stakeholder Group | Communication Method | Purpose | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Procurement | Workshops and interviews | Understand purchasing issues and validate requirements | During discovery and requirements validation |
| Warehouse | Process observation and workshops | Understand goods receipt and stock control activities | During discovery and process design |
| Finance | Interviews and review sessions | Confirm invoice matching rules and financial controls | During requirements and UAT preparation |
| Supply Chain Management | Progress meetings and KPI reviews | Confirm priorities and review expected benefits | At agreed project milestones |
| IT / ERP Team | Requirements reviews and solution discussions | Assess feasibility, dependencies and risks | Throughout analysis and design |
| UAT Participants | Test briefings and feedback sessions | Validate the proposed process against acceptance criteria | During UAT |

## 5. Business Analyst Responsibilities

The Business Analyst should identify relevant stakeholders, understand their needs, facilitate discussions and document agreed requirements.

The analyst should also manage conflicting priorities, confirm decisions, communicate changes and maintain traceability between business objectives, requirements and testing.

For example, Warehouse may prefer a simple receiving process, while Finance may require additional checks to maintain accurate records. The Business Analyst should document both needs and facilitate agreement on a process that balances operational efficiency with financial control.

## 6. Stakeholder Risks

- Different departments may have conflicting priorities.
- Some users may be concerned about changes to established working practices.
- Requirements may be incomplete if key users are not consulted.
- Delayed stakeholder decisions may affect delivery timelines.
- Insufficient UAT participation may leave important scenarios untested.

These risks should be managed through early engagement, clear documentation, agreed decision ownership and structured validation.

## 7. Conclusion

Effective stakeholder engagement helps ensure that the proposed solution reflects real business needs rather than assumptions. The Business Analyst acts as a link between business users, decision makers and the technical team to support a shared understanding of the problem and the proposed improvements.
