# Requirements Workshop: Supply Chain Process Improvement

## 1. Workshop Purpose

The purpose of this workshop is to understand the current procurement, goods receipt, inventory and supplier invoice processes in a fictional manufacturing company.

The workshop will identify business problems, gather stakeholder needs, clarify business rules and agree the next steps for requirements validation.

## 2. Workshop Objectives

- Understand the current business processes.
- Identify operational challenges and their root causes.
- Gather functional and non-functional requirements.
- Identify exceptions, dependencies and business rules.
- Agree priorities and success measures.
- Confirm outstanding questions and actions.

## 3. Participants

The proposed participants are the Procurement Manager, Procurement Officer, Warehouse Manager, Warehouse Operative, Finance Manager, Accounts Payable Officer, Supply Chain Manager and IT / ERP representative.

The Business Analyst facilitates the workshop and documents the discussion, decisions, assumptions and actions.

## 4. Discovery Questions

### Procurement

1. How are purchase orders currently created and approved?
2. How do users check the status of an outstanding purchase order?
3. How are supplier delivery dates recorded and updated?
4. What happens when a supplier cannot deliver the full quantity?
5. Which purchasing activities take the most time?

### Warehouse and Inventory

1. How are incoming goods checked against purchase orders?
2. How are received quantities recorded?
3. How are damaged, missing or incorrect items handled?
4. How are partial deliveries recorded and monitored?
5. How do users investigate differences between physical stock and system records?

### Finance and Accounts Payable

1. How are supplier invoices checked against purchase orders and goods receipts?
2. What types of invoice discrepancies occur most frequently?
3. Who investigates and approves exceptions?
4. Are there agreed tolerances for quantity or price differences?
5. What controls must be completed before an invoice can be approved for payment?

### Supply Chain Management

1. Which supply chain performance measures are currently monitored?
2. What information is missing from existing reports?
3. How quickly should users identify overdue deliveries?
4. Which operational problems should be prioritised?
5. How will success be measured after the proposed changes?

### IT / ERP Team

1. Which ERP system and relevant modules are currently in use?
2. Which requirements can be supported by existing functionality?
3. What configuration, integration or reporting dependencies exist?
4. What security roles and audit requirements apply?
5. What data quality, migration or testing risks need to be considered?

## 5. Example Workshop Scenario

A supplier delivers 80 units against a purchase order for 100 units. The warehouse records the received quantity, but the supplier invoice arrives for all 100 units.

The Business Analyst should ask:

- How should the partial delivery be recorded?
- Should the outstanding 20 units remain open on the purchase order?
- Should the invoice be held, partially processed or handled through another approved process?
- Who investigates the discrepancy?
- What evidence is required to resolve it?
- What should happen when the remaining goods arrive?

The answers must be confirmed with the relevant business stakeholders and validated against the organisation's ERP configuration and financial controls.

## 6. Expected Workshop Outputs

- Validated current-state process information.
- A list of business problems and root cause hypotheses.
- Draft functional and non-functional requirements.
- Agreed business rules and exception scenarios.
- A prioritised list of requirements.
- Outstanding questions, assigned actions and decision owners.
- Initial acceptance criteria and proposed success measures.

## 7. Follow-Up Actions

After the workshop, the Business Analyst should circulate the notes to participants, confirm that the requirements accurately reflect the discussion, resolve open questions and update the requirements and traceability documents.

Any assumptions should remain clearly labelled until stakeholders validate them.

## 8. Conclusion

A structured requirements workshop helps the Business Analyst develop a shared understanding of business needs before solution design begins. Asking open questions, exploring exceptions and documenting decisions reduces the risk of incomplete or misunderstood requirements.
