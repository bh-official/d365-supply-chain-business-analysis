# Project Implementation Roadmap: Supply Chain Process Improvement

## 1. Purpose

This roadmap outlines the proposed phases for improving procurement, goods receipt, inventory management and supplier invoice processing at a fictional manufacturing company.

It illustrates how a Business Analyst works with stakeholders, project managers and technical teams throughout the project lifecycle.

This is a proposed roadmap for a portfolio case study, not a schedule for a live ERP implementation.

## 2. Project Phases

| Phase | Key Activities | Business Analyst Deliverables | Exit Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | Understand the business problem, objectives and stakeholders | Business case and stakeholder register | Business problem and objectives agreed |
| 2. Current-State Analysis | Review existing workflows, pain points and available data | As-is process map and problem analysis | Current process validated by stakeholders |
| 3. Requirements Definition | Conduct workshops, gather requirements and clarify business rules | Business requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria | Requirements reviewed and prioritised |
| 4. Gap Analysis and Solution Assessment | Compare current and desired processes; assess available ERP capabilities | Gap analysis, change impact analysis and recommendations | Gaps and proposed approach reviewed |
| 5. Solution Design and Planning | Work with the technical team to assess configuration, reporting and integration needs | Validated requirements, process design and traceability updates | Design and scope approved by authorised stakeholders |
| 6. Configuration and Development | Technical team configures or develops the agreed solution | Requirements clarification and change control support | Agreed solution components are ready for testing |
| 7. System and Integration Testing | Verify system behaviour and interactions between components | Requirements clarification and test scenario review | Defined testing exit criteria met |
| 8. User Acceptance Testing | Business users validate the solution against agreed requirements | UAT test cases, defect log and sign-off evidence | Business acceptance criteria satisfied and authorised sign-off obtained |
| 9. Deployment Preparation | Prepare training, communications, data readiness and support arrangements | Business readiness checklist and process documentation | Deployment readiness approved |
| 10. Go-Live and Support | Release the solution and monitor early operational performance | Issue tracking, KPI monitoring and feedback collection | Agreed support and handover criteria met |
| 11. Benefits Review | Compare actual results with baselines and agreed targets | KPI analysis and lessons learned | Benefits reviewed with stakeholders |

## 3. Example Milestone Plan

The following is an illustrative sequence. Actual dates and durations would depend on project scope, complexity, resources and delivery approach.

| Milestone | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|
| M1: Discovery Complete | Business problem and objectives agreed |
| M2: Requirements Approved | Prioritised requirements and acceptance criteria agreed |
| M3: Solution Design Approved | Proposed design and dependencies reviewed |
| M4: Testing Complete | Required testing completed and outstanding defects assessed |
| M5: Business Readiness Approved | Users, processes and support arrangements prepared |
| M6: Go-Live Approved | Authorised decision makers approve deployment |
| M7: Benefits Review Complete | Performance assessed against the agreed baseline and targets |

## 4. Key Dependencies

- Stakeholders must be available to validate requirements and decisions.
- Business rules for partial deliveries and invoice discrepancies must be agreed.
- Relevant master data and transaction data must be sufficiently accurate.
- The IT / ERP team must validate solution feasibility and dependencies.
- Testing environments, test data and appropriate user access must be available.
- Deployment must follow the organisation's approval and change management procedures.

## 5. Business Analyst Responsibilities Across the Lifecycle

The Business Analyst supports discovery, requirements elicitation, process analysis, stakeholder communication, prioritisation and traceability.

During solution design, the analyst clarifies business needs and works with technical specialists to assess whether the proposed solution meets them.

During testing, the analyst helps ensure that requirements are covered by appropriate test scenarios and that business users can validate the solution.

After deployment, the analyst may help review feedback, investigate process issues and assess whether the expected business benefits have been achieved.

The Business Analyst does not automatically own technical configuration, development, deployment approval or project management responsibilities. These depend on the organisation and project structure.

## 6. Delivery Risks

- Delayed stakeholder decisions may affect the timeline.
- Changes to requirements may increase scope or testing effort.
- Poor data quality may delay testing or deployment.
- Inadequate UAT coverage may leave important business scenarios untested.
- Insufficient user training may affect adoption.
- Benefits may be difficult to measure without a reliable baseline.

These risks should be monitored and managed with the relevant project and business owners.

## 7. Success Measures

The project should establish a baseline and agree measurable targets for inventory accuracy, goods receipt processing time, supplier invoice discrepancy rate, exception resolution time and on-time delivery.

Performance should be reviewed after implementation using reliable data and agreed reporting methods.

## 8. Conclusion

A structured implementation roadmap connects business analysis activities with the wider delivery lifecycle. It provides visibility of milestones, dependencies, responsibilities and acceptance criteria while helping stakeholders understand what must happen before the solution can go live.
