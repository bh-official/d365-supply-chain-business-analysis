# Risk and Issue Register: Supply Chain Process Improvement

## 1. Purpose

This register records potential risks and existing issues that could affect the proposed supply chain process improvement project.

A risk is an uncertain event that may occur in the future. An issue is a problem that has already occurred and needs to be addressed.

The entries below are illustrative examples for a fictional manufacturing company.

## 2. Risk Register

| ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Priority | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-001 | Inaccurate inventory data affects reporting and stock decisions | High | High | High | Assess data quality, investigate discrepancies and agree validation controls |
| R-002 | Stakeholders provide conflicting requirements | Medium | High | High | Facilitate workshops, document decisions and obtain stakeholder agreement |
| R-003 | Users resist changes to existing receiving procedures | Medium | Medium | Medium | Involve users early, provide training and gather feedback |
| R-004 | UAT does not cover important exception scenarios | Medium | High | High | Map requirements to test cases and review coverage with stakeholders |
| R-005 | ERP configuration does not support an assumed business process | Medium | High | High | Validate requirements with the IT / ERP team before confirming the solution |
| R-006 | Reporting requirements are unclear or inconsistent | Medium | Medium | Medium | Agree KPI definitions, data sources, ownership and reporting frequency |

## 3. Issue Register

| ID | Issue | Business Impact | Proposed Action | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I-001 | The current process documentation does not explain how partial deliveries are handled | Requirements may be incomplete | Interview warehouse and procurement users and document the agreed process | Open |
| I-002 | Ownership of invoice discrepancy investigation is unclear | Exceptions may remain unresolved | Confirm responsibilities and escalation procedures with Finance | Open |
| I-003 | KPI baseline data has not yet been established | Benefits cannot be measured reliably | Identify suitable historical data and agree the baseline calculation | Open |

These issues are examples for the portfolio and have not been verified against a real organisation.

## 4. Risk Assessment Method

Likelihood and impact should be assessed using agreed project criteria.

For this example:

- High likelihood means the event is considered relatively likely to occur.
- Medium likelihood means the event is possible.
- Low likelihood means the event is considered unlikely.
- High impact means significant consequences for business operations, controls, scope or delivery.
- Medium impact means a manageable but meaningful disruption.
- Low impact means limited consequences.

The project team should agree the detailed scoring criteria and review them consistently.

## 5. Risk Ownership and Monitoring

Each risk should have a named owner responsible for monitoring it and coordinating mitigation actions.

The Business Analyst may maintain the register, facilitate discussions and highlight dependencies, but the relevant business or technical owner remains accountable for managing their assigned risk.

The register should be reviewed at agreed project meetings and updated when new information becomes available.

## 6. Escalation

High priority risks should be raised promptly with the appropriate project manager or decision maker, particularly where they could affect financial controls, business operations, delivery commitments or regulatory obligations.

Issues should be assigned to an appropriate owner with an agreed action and target resolution date.

## 7. Conclusion

A maintained risk and issue register helps the project team identify problems early, clarify ownership and take action before risks develop into larger delivery or operational problems.
